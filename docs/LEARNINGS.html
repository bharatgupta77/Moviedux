Recommendation Systems

TF-IDF vs SVD — Two Ways to Recommend Movies

Both are recommendation algorithms but they answer completely different questions. MovieDux uses both, side by side, on the Watchlist page.

TF-IDF  Phase 2

TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency. Fancy name, simple idea: turn each movie's text (title + genre + overview) into a bag of weighted words, then find movies whose word bags look most alike.

TF (Term Frequency) — how often a word appears in this movie's description.
IDF (Inverse Document Frequency) — how rare that word is across all movies. Common words like "the" get near-zero weight; rare words like "heist" or "dystopian" get high weight.

Real Example from MovieDux

You add Inception to your watchlist.

TF-IDF sees its keywords: dream • heist • subconscious • layers • thriller

It scans every other movie and finds the ones with the most overlap in those words. Result: Shutter Island, Memento, The Prestige

It never cared that people love these movies. It only cared about the words.

SVD — Collaborative Filtering  Phase 3

SVD stands for Singular Value Decomposition — a matrix math technique. Forget the movie description entirely. Build a giant table of who rated what, then find hidden patterns in that table.

The core idea: users who liked the same movies as you, probably like the movies you haven't seen yet. SVD compresses the ratings table into hidden "taste dimensions" and uses them to predict your missing ratings.

Real Example

You and User B both rated Inception ★5, Shutter Island ★4, Memento ★5 — nearly identical taste.

User B also rated Tenet ★4. You haven't seen it.

SVD predicts you'd rate Tenet ~4/5 and recommends it. It never read what Tenet is about — it just saw the pattern.

In MovieDux, Phase 3 seeds the model with synthetic ratings generated from TMDB popularity and genre data. Phase 4 will replace these with actual user ratings.

TF-IDF (Content-Based)

  • Based on Movie's words & genres
  • Asks What movies are similar to this movie?
  • Needs ratings? No
  • Blind spot Ignores what humans actually feel
  • Phase Phase 2

SVD (Collaborative)

  • Based on Human rating patterns
  • Asks What do people like you enjoy?
  • Needs ratings? Yes
  • Blind spot Needs enough data to be accurate
  • Phase Phase 3
💡

Why use both? TF-IDF works immediately — no data needed. SVD gets smarter over time as more users rate movies. Together they cover each other's blind spots: TF-IDF handles new movies nobody has rated yet; SVD surfaces hidden gems that don't sound similar on paper but people with your taste consistently love. On the Watchlist page, "Recommended For You" is TF-IDF and "You Might Also Like" is SVD — two different signals on the same screen.
